🔎 Concept · Headline Framing

Headlines are written to make you click — not always to tell you the truth.

Publishers often exaggerate, dramatize, or leave out context in headlines because attention pays the bills. A headline can be technically true and still create a completely wrong impression.

Why it matters: If you only ever read headlines (most people do), an exaggerated one can shape your entire understanding of an event — and that misunderstanding spreads every time you share it.

❤️ Concept · Emotional Manipulation

Content that makes you feel something spreads faster than content that informs you.

Outrage, fear, pride, and awe are the emotions most likely to make you stop scrolling, comment, or share. Creators and algorithms both reward posts that trigger strong feelings — regardless of accuracy.

Why it matters: When you can name the emotion a post is pulling on, you regain the ability to pause before reacting — instead of the content reacting for you.
