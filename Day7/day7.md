# My Claude Usage Strategy — Azad Singh

## Recommended Primary Model
**Claude Sonnet 5**

## Why This Model Fits Me
- You use Claude **daily**, for a mix of **learning (ITIL, certs)** and **creative work** — that's a "reasoning + writing quality" combo, not a "cheapest/fastest" combo, so Haiku is underpowered and Opus is overkill for most days.
- Sonnet is Anthropic's balanced tier: strong writing voice, solid reasoning for concept explanations, and fast enough for daily back-and-forth (e.g. your 60 Days of Claude posts, ITIL explanations).
- You're not doing heavy coding or multi-hour agentic research — so you don't need Opus-tier "grind through complexity" horsepower every day. Save that for specific moments (below).

## When to Use Haiku
- Quick lookups, definitions, "what does this ITIL term mean," rewriting a sentence, fast LinkedIn caption tweaks.
- Anytime you want a near-instant answer and don't need depth or a polished creative voice.

## When to Use Sonnet
- Your daily driver: ITIL concept explanations, study notes, 60-day challenge write-ups, LinkedIn posts, drafting reflections, general "help me think this through" conversations.
- Anything creative that still needs coherence and a consistent voice across multiple paragraphs.

## When to Use Opus
- When a creative piece needs to be **your best work** — a capstone post, a portfolio piece, a keynote-style write-up, or content you'll publish somewhere high-visibility (recruiters, LinkedIn thought-leadership).
- Complex, layered explanations (e.g., comparing ITIL v4 practices against real incident-management scenarios) where you want maximum nuance.
- Not needed for routine daily posts — reserve it for "this one really matters."

## Recommended Effort Level

### Low
Rare for you — only for throwaway quick facts (e.g. "what's the ITIL term for X").

### Standard
**Your default.** Good for daily learning Q&A, routine LinkedIn drafts, general challenge write-ups.

### High
Use when: writing a creative piece you'll actually publish, working through a nuanced ITIL scenario, or want Claude to really think before responding (e.g. "explain this like I'll be tested on it").

### Max
Save for rare, high-stakes creative or career moments — a resume rewrite for a big application, a flagship post you want to be exceptional, or a complex strategic write-up.

## My Personalized Claude Workflow

| Task | Best Model | Best Effort | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daily "60 Days of Claude" post draft | Sonnet | Standard | Fast, consistent voice, no need for max depth daily |
| ITIL v4 concept study/explanation | Sonnet | High | Needs clear, nuanced reasoning to actually stick |
| Quick term lookup / fact check | Haiku | Low | Speed over depth |
| Flagship/publish-worthy creative post | Opus | High–Max | Quality and polish matter most |
| Career prep (resume, cover letter, interview prep) | Opus | High | High stakes, needs your best possible output |
| Brainstorming challenge topics | Sonnet | Standard | Fast ideation, doesn't need heavy reasoning |
| Reflective/personal writing for the challenge | Sonnet | Standard–High | Voice and coherence matter more than raw power |

## Biggest Mistakes I Should Avoid
- **Defaulting to Opus for everything** — burns time/cost on routine tasks that Sonnet handles just as well.
- **Using Max effort out of habit** — it's meant for genuinely hard or high-stakes moments, not daily posts.
- **Using Haiku for anything creative** — it's built for speed, not voice or depth, and your challenge content deserves better.
- **Treating "effort" as a magic quality button** — it nudges reasoning depth, but a vague prompt at Max effort still gets you a mediocre answer. Clear, specific prompts matter more than cranking effort up.

## Final Recommendation
**If you could pick only ONE model + ONE effort level for most of your work: Claude Sonnet 5 at Standard effort.**

It matches your actual daily pattern — a working professional doing daily learning and creative writing, not heavy coding or research. It's fast enough to keep pace with a daily habit, but capable enough to give your ITIL explanations real depth and your posts a genuine voice. Reserve Opus + High/Max for the handful of moments each month where the output really needs to be your best — flagship posts, career documents, or complex strategic write-ups.
