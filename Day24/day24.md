Day 24 — 60 Days of Claude: Business Validation Sprint

Focus: Turning yesterday's validated pivot into a full AI co-founder strategy report.

What I did
Took the Day 23 output (Customer & MVP Blueprint + Startup Validation Report for the "Major-Incident Pattern Detection for SMB IT Service Desks" pivot) and fed it back into Claude with a new role: AI Co-Founder / YC Advisor / Business Strategist.
Prompted for evidence over assumptions: who pays, why they pay, how they'll discover the product, and the weakest untested assumptions in the plan.
Generated a full business strategy report: Business Model Canvas, Revenue & Pricing Strategy, GTM Strategy, Customer Acquisition Strategy, First 100 Users Plan, Competitive Position & Moat, and a Reverse SWOT.
Had Claude score the idea on 5 investor-relevant dimensions (0–100) instead of accepting a single "looks good" verdict.
Built a one-page visual dashboard (SVG infographic) summarizing the business model, GTM flow, moat, risks, and scorecard.
Exported everything as a formatted, print-ready PDF report.
Key output
Dimension	Score	Why
Business Viability	45/100	Real pain + concierge-first path, but no paying customers yet
Revenue Potential	35/100	Low ACV, pricing untested against real buyers
GTM Strength	40/100	Channel hypotheses sound, zero qualified conversations so far
Competitive Strength	50/100	Underserved wedge, but weak structural moat
Investor Readiness	20/100	Pre-revenue, pre-validation, solo non-technical founder

Composite score: 38/100 → 🟡 Validate, don't build yet.

Lesson learned

An AI co-founder is most useful when it refuses to be a cheerleader. The most valuable part of today's output wasn't the polished report — it was the list of weak assumptions to validate before writing any code: whether "pattern detection" actually reads as different from "triage" to a real buyer, and whether the $100–500/month price survives an actual sales conversation.
