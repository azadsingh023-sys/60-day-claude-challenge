DAY 5
Publish Post #2 — lessons-learned / hot take
›
Hot take: the most useful skill I brought into Network Engineering wasn't a networking skill at all — it was 12 years of Incident Management. Most network issues aren't hard because of the technology. They're hard because five people are messaging you at once, the business wants an ETA you don't have yet, and you're troubleshooting under pressure. ITSM taught me how to stay calm during an outage, communicate status without overpromising, and document the fix so it doesn't happen twice. Cisco Meraki and Fortinet taught me the "how." ITSM taught me the "how to handle it when it breaks at 9am on a Monday." If you're moving from a service/support background into a hands-on infrastructure role — don't undersell the incident-response muscle you already built. It's rarer than the technical skill, and it's exactly what keeps a network team calm during a real outage.

DAY 6
Engage with every comment + 5 more connections

DAY 7
Review profile views & connection acceptance rate
›

What the numbers mean:

  • Profile views spike after posting or commenting — that's the algorithm surfacing you, and it confirms the headline/photo are doing their job of making people click through.
  • Low connection-acceptance rate (under ~40%) usually means your targeting is off, not your message — you may be reaching too senior or too unrelated a group.
  • High profile views but no messages often means the About section still isn't closing — that's a sign to revisit the hook and CTA lines.

Adjust: if views are up but connections are flat, tighten your Day 3 targeting list. If connections are up but engagement is quiet, your posting cadence (Day 2 & 5) needs to continue weekly, not just this one week.
