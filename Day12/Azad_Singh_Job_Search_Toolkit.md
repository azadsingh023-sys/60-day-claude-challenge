## SECTION 12: Interview Talking Points

**Most Impressive Achievements**
- 12+ years of sustained, uninterrupted focus on incident and service management, across seven organizations — a strong signal of specialization and depth, not generalist drift
- Genuine multi-region global support experience (APAC, Middle East, EMEA, US, UK) — rare on paper and highly relevant to a role explicitly requiring 24x7 global coverage
- Range across the stack: service desk engineer → incident lifecycle manager → infrastructure/access lead → network engineer, showing you understand incidents from both the process side and the technical root-cause side

**Key Stories to Discuss**
- A specific major incident you managed end-to-end at Haldor Topsoe, Universal Weather & Aviation, or SOTI — walk through detection, escalation, stakeholder communication, and resolution
- An example of a knowledge article or documentation improvement you created that measurably reduced repeat tickets or resolution time
- A cross-functional coordination story — working with SMEs, engineering, or vendor teams (SOTI's EMM/MDM support is a strong example) to resolve an incident under SLA pressure
- Your transition into network engineering at GLA University — frame this as evidence you can go deeper technically than a typical ITSM manager, which strengthens your credibility on RCA

**Areas Recruiters Are Likely to Ask About**
- The 2022–2024 career gap — have a brief, confident, forward-looking answer ready (e.g., "I took planned time for personal health reasons and used it to stay current with ITIL, Microsoft 365, and ServiceNow; I'm fully recovered and have been back in continuous, demanding roles since 2024")
- Whether you've formally owned Problem Management (RCA/CAPA) as a named process versus as part of incident management — be ready to describe informal RCA work you've done and your plan to formalize it (see Skill Gap Analysis)
- Direct people management experience — clarify what "mentoring" or "coordinating cross-functional teams" looked like in practice, even without formal direct reports
- Familiarity with PagerDuty, Datadog, Centreon, or Pingdom — be honest that your tooling is ServiceNow/Jira/Salesforce, but pivot to how quickly you've picked up new platforms throughout your career (7 employers, 7 different environments)
- Why the move from Network Engineer (GLA University) back to a Service Manager-style role — frame it as returning to your core strength (ITSM leadership) after broadening your technical depth
